# Content and speaker notes

## Research and provenance

Start from the full relevant source set rather than a hand-picked example. Keep verified facts separate from inference and preserve source paths, URLs, commit hashes, ticket keys, or dates that make important claims traceable.

For engineering reviews, organize the story by delivered behavior, technical area, or decision. Avoid individual performance scorecards unless the user explicitly requests them. Git history is evidence, not ready-made slide copy.

## Slide writing

Use direct, concrete language. Prefer a short subject title for setup and process slides. Use a takeaway title only when the evidence on the slide supports it.

Avoid filler slogans, vague abstractions, repeated subtitles, and decorative labels that do not add meaning. Keep body copy short enough to read at presentation distance. Move explanation into notes before shrinking text.

## Speaker notes

Notes should sound natural when read aloud. Include:

1. The context the audience needs.
2. The main point supported by the slide.
3. A visual cue when the audience should look at a particular image or value.
4. A transition to the next slide when useful.

Do not copy the visible bullets into notes. Do not include creation commentary, validation logs, or private credentials. Put source references at the end of the notes when research informed the slide.
